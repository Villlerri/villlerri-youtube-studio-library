Part one. Dusk.
It is a Saturday in the middle of January, in the year eighteen ninety-two. You are on the coast of Maine, at the place where the Penobscot River opens into the bay.
There is a small light station here, on a point of land called Fort Point. And for this one night, you are its keeper.
You have one task before the day ends. When the sun goes down, the lamp at the top of the tower must be burning.
Nothing else is required of you. There is no hurry. There is only the work, and the evening, and the long winter night that comes after.
So let the day settle around you. Let your shoulders drop. We will walk through this evening slowly, one small thing at a time.
The sun is down now. The water has gone dark, and the sky over the far shore is the color of ashes.
The book says the lamp must be placed inside the lens, in its place, and then lighted.
So you set it down, gently, at the very center of all that glass.
You strike a match. The wick takes the flame.
And now comes the part that cannot be hurried.
For the first half hour, the flame must be kept low. The glass chimney of the lamp has to warm slowly. If you turn the wick up too soon, before the chimney and the burner are warm, the lamp will smoke.
So you keep it low. A small flame, in the middle of the great glass.
And slowly, the lens begins to fill. Ring after ring takes the light, until the whole lantern glows with it.
